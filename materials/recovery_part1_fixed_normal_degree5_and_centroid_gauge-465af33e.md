## 1. Fixed normal data and the actual convex polyhedron

Let n_i be fixed nonzero rational outward normals, h_i support parameters, and
P(h) = {x in R^3 : n_i dot x <= h_i for every i}.
Require P to be bounded, full-dimensional, and every listed plane to be a distinct maximal facet. This is actual Euclidean halfspace data. An abstract oriented cell, a positive circuit, or an intrinsic metric is not assumed to supply it automatically.

Inside a fixed face-lattice chamber, choose three independent incident planes at every vertex. Each vertex v(h) is linear in h by the fixed 3x3 inverse. For nonsimple vertices the extra incidences are linear equalities on h and must be retained. Nonincidence conditions are strict linear inequalities. All degree assertions below hold only in such a chamber, not when the normals themselves vary.

Choose a fixed outward triangulation of each maximal facet. For the oriented boundary triangles (a,b,c), define
D(h) = sum det(a,b,c),
Q(h) = sum det(a,b,c) (a+b+c).
Signed tetrahedral integration gives volume V=D/6>0, first moment M=Q/24 and own solid centroid g=Q/(4D). The formula remains valid when the coordinate origin is outside P, because the tetrahedral integrals are signed. It is not the arithmetic mean of vertices or the centroid of the surface.

D is homogeneous of degree 3 in h, Q of degree 4. Set
W_v = 4D v - Q,
f_i = 4D h_i - n_i dot Q.
Both have degree 4. The centroid is strictly interior, so all f_i>0.

## 2. Facet predicates, including weak cases

For i != j define
A_ij = (n_i dot n_i) f_j - (n_i dot n_j) f_i.
The perpendicular foot on plane i is
q_i = g + ((h_i - n_i dot g)/(n_i dot n_i)) n_i.
Direct substitution gives
h_j - n_j dot q_i = A_ij / (4D (n_i dot n_i)).
Consequently:
- facet i is strictly stable exactly when all A_ij>0;
- its foot is a boundary/weak supporting equilibrium exactly when all A_ij>=0 and at least one is zero;
- the facet has no supporting foot, strict or weak, exactly when at least one A_ij<0.

Each A_ij has degree 4. A zero in one comparison is not itself a weak equilibrium when another comparison is negative: that foot is outside the facet. The implementation retains this disjunction instead of demanding that every algebraic comparison everywhere be nonzero.

## 3. Vertex predicates without a simplicity assumption

For each actual neighboring vertex w of v, set
B_vw = W_v dot (v-w).
The tangent cone at v is generated by the incident edge directions w-v. Hence its polar normal cone consists of radial directions satisfying every B_vw>=0. The radial vector v-g is in the interior of that cone exactly when every B_vw>0.

Thus all positive means strictly unstable; all nonnegative with a zero means a weak vertex equilibrium; one negative excludes both. The degree is 5. Testing every neighbor handles nonsimple vertices without choosing possibly misleading coefficients in a redundant normal generating set.

## 4. Edge predicates and complete weak exclusion

For an actual edge [a,b] with its two maximal incident facets i,j, set
e=b-a, L=e dot e>0,
a_i=n_i dot n_i, a_j=n_j dot n_j, c=n_i dot n_j,
Delta=a_i a_j-c^2>0,
T=-W_a dot e,
R_i=a_j f_i-c f_j,
R_j=a_i f_j-c f_i.

The foot on the edge line is a+t e with t=T/(4DL). Since the radial vector at this foot is perpendicular to e, it lies in span(n_i,n_j). Solving its two scalar products gives normal-cone coefficients
alpha_i=R_i/(4D Delta), alpha_j=R_j/(4D Delta).

The edge has a strict saddle exactly when every member of
[T, 4DL-T, R_i, R_j]
is positive. A closed supporting candidate exists exactly when all four are nonnegative. A zero then indicates a boundary case: a foot at an endpoint, a radial direction on a cone boundary, or both. These correspond to genuine weak supporting equilibria, although a single physical point can be represented by several strata.

To exclude every strict and weak edge candidate, require at least one of the four entries to be strictly negative. The first two entries have degree 5, the last two degree 4. Also R_j=A_ij and R_i=A_ji for this facet pair.

The implementation's weak_stratum_flags are flags counted by represented strata, NOT numbers of distinct physical weak equilibrium points. Their simultaneous vanishing is the needed no-weak predicate. The index identity S+U-H=2 is only an additional consistency test and is not used to infer absence of weak equilibria.

## 5. Finite strict-polynomial formulation of the target

Fix one proposed stable facet i* and one proposed unstable vertex v*. Impose:
(a) all A_i*j>0, and for each other facet choose at least one A_ij<0;
(b) all B_v*w>0, and for each other vertex choose at least one B_vw<0;
(c) for every edge choose at least one negative entry in its four-entry test.
Together with the actual geometric chamber conditions, these are necessary and sufficient for strict own-centroid class110 without weak equilibria. The various choices of exclusion witnesses form a finite union of basic open semialgebraic systems with polynomial degree at most 5. ## 6. Centroid derivative and the translation-gauge trap

For physical facet area S_i and its area centroid c_i, moving h_i by dh_i displaces plane i by normal distance dh_i/||n_i||. First-order prism integration gives
dV/dh_i = S_i/||n_i||,
dM/dh_i = (S_i/||n_i||) c_i,
J_i := dg/dh_i = S_i (c_i-g)/(V ||n_i||).

The expressions can be computed rationally without square roots. For an outward facet triangle (a,b,c), its area divided by ||n_i|| is
w = ((b-a) cross (c-a)) dot n_i / (2 (n_i dot n_i)).
Sum these positive rational w and weight the triangle centroid (a+b+c)/3 to recover S_i/||n_i|| and c_i.

Let N be the m-by-3 matrix whose rows are n_i^T and let J have columns J_i. A whole-body translation by t sends h to h+Nt and sends g to g+t. Therefore
J N = I_3.
An unrestricted centroid Jacobian necessarily has rank three for EVERY such full-dimensional polyhedron. It is not evidence of an intrinsic shape adjustment that moves the centroid relative to an equilibrium-bearing boundary. Similarly, uniform scaling gives J h = g. Both identities are checked exactly by part 3.

To discuss real shape control, first remove the translational freedom, for example by fixing three independent support planes, and then impose every additional incidence and margin constraint on the allowed support perturbations. Only the restricted Jacobian is informative. In the exact regular-tetrahedron test its rank drops from 3 to 1 after three-plane pinning; in the cube test it remains 3. This contrast illustrates the gauge problem, not a route around the tetrahedral lower bounds.

Even rank three after proper pinning yields only a local inverse-function statement. It does not prove that a distant desired center can be reached without crossing a face-lattice wall or creating an extra equilibrium. For a nonsimple body, extra linear incidence constraints can reduce the allowed rank further. Any continuation must track the actual allowed directions and the strict polynomial margins. This is a methodological warning, not an allegation that an unread earlier branch made this error.
