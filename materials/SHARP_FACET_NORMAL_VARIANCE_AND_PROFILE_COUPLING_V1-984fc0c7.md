## 1. Definitions and identities

Let P be a full-dimensional compact convex d-body, uniformly filled, with actual centroid0, volume V and covariance Sigma=E_P xx^T. Let F be a genuine positive-area facet, P subset{q.x<=1}. Set

w=Area(F)/||q||, alpha=w/V, c=E_F x, T=E_F xx^T.

Here E_F is normalized (d-1)-dimensional uniform area on the complete facet, not a selected integration triangle. T is positive definite, Tq=c, q.c=1, c^T T^-1 c=1. Define

B=alpha^-1 T^-1-qq^T.

The archived maximum-volume prescribed-centroid theorem identifies this with -Hess log V(z) at a regular active completion of a fixed released kernel. The matrix inequalities in this note are statements about the actual P,F moments themselves; they do not require changing the centre or moving another facet. Its inverse is

B^-1=alpha T + alpha^2/(1-alpha) cc^T.

All inequalities are affine-covariant when body coordinates and covectors are transformed consistently. Physical M,N=M^-1 remain a separate single common metric, not independent choices generated by these bounds.

### Elementary true-facet mass bound

For t=q.x the actual probability density rho(t) has support[a,1], mean0, integral1 and endpoint rho(1)=alpha. Brunn-Minkowski gives concavity of g=rho^(1/(d-1)) on its support when d>=2. Compare to the pyramid density with the same upper endpoint and endpoint value:

rho0(t)=alpha ((t-a0)/(1-a0))^(d-1) on[a0,1], a0=1-d/alpha.

Its mass is1 and its mean is 1-d/[alpha(d+1)]. If a<a0, g(a0)>0 and concavity, together with equality at1, imply g>g0 on the interior[a0,1]; the extra left support then contradicts equal masses. Thus a>=a0. On the common support g-g0 is concave and vanishes at1. Once it is positive it stays positive up to1. Including the initial model-only interval, rho-rho0 has at most one sign change, from negative to positive. Equal masses therefore imply mean(rho)>=mean(rho0). Since the actual mean is0,

0<alpha<=d/(d+1).

Equality forces the pyramid normal density. This also follows from the affine subspace concentration theorem; the elementary argument suffices here. The d=1 interval case can be calculated directly.

## 2. A new full-matrix bound, with its exact limitation

The origin-cone endpoint probability measure is

nu0=sum_f (alpha_f/d) sigma_f,

where sigma_f is normalized true-facet area, alpha_f=Area(F_f)/(||q_f||V). The divergence theorem gives

sum alpha_f=d, E_nu0 x=0, E_nu0 xx^T=(d+2)Sigma/d.

Remove the component (alpha/d)sigma_F. The remaining positive measure has mass1-alpha/d, first moment-(alpha/d)c and second moment (d+2)Sigma/d-(alpha/d)T. Positivity of its covariance proves

(d+2)Sigma >= alpha T + alpha^2/(d-alpha) cc^T.                 (2.1)

Put p=T^-1/2 c, so ||p||=1. Under the same congruence, the right side of (2.1) has tangent eigenvalue alpha and normal eigenvalue alpha*d/(d-alpha), while T^1/2 B T^1/2=alpha^-1 I-pp^T. Comparing inverses yields

B >= k_d(alpha) Sigma^-1,
k_d(alpha)=d(1-alpha)/[(d+2)(d-alpha)] >=1/[d(d+2)].           (2.2)

The final inequality uses alpha<=d/(d+1) and the monotonicity of k_d. Thus B>=Sigma^-1/15 in3D. No assertion of optimality is made for this full-matrix constant. The desired coefficient1/5 would instead require

5Sigma >= alpha T+alpha^2/(1-alpha)cc^T,                      (2.3)

and (2.1) has denominator3-alpha, not1-alpha. These expressions must not be conflated. Endpoint positivity by itself has not proved (2.3).

## 3. Sharp normal variance at every facet mass in3D

For r>=0 define

A(r)=3(3r^2+2r+1)/[4(r^2+r+1)^2],
S(r)=3(r^4+4r^3+10r^2+4r+1)/[5(3r^2+2r+1)^2].

A(0)=3/4, A(r) tends to0 as r tends to infinity, and

A'(r)=-9r^2(r+1)/[2(r^2+r+1)^3]<0 for r>0.

Every actual alpha in(0,3/4] therefore determines a unique r>=0.

**Theorem.** For every genuine facet of an actual uniformly filled centred convex3-body,

q^T Sigma q >= S(r), where A(r)=alpha.                       (3.1)

For each alpha this bound is attained by a homothetic frustum, with the selected facet the top base. It is not merely an optimized cone-law inequality.

### Exact comparator

Take a reference family whose sectional area is proportional to ell(z)^2 on0<=z<=1, where ell(z)=r+(1-r)z. Its integral, mean and top-centroid distance are

v=(r^2+r+1)/3,
m=(r^2+2r+3)/[4(r^2+r+1)],
h=1-m=(3r^2+2r+1)/[4(r^2+r+1)].

Use t=(z-m)/h. The resulting reference density rho0 has mass1, mean0, top endpoint1 and rho0(1)=h/v=A(r). Its variance is S(r). Its square root g0 is positive affine on its support[a0,1], and is zero outside that interval. At r=0 it vanishes at a0. A square-base frustum realizes this reference as an actual uniform3D normal marginal; any convex2D base works.

### Complete comparison proof

Let rho be the actual marginal and g=sqrt(rho), concave on[a,1]. The endpoint values g(1)=g0(1)=sqrt(alpha) agree. On the common interval, h0=g-g0 is concave and h0(1)=0. If h0 is positive at one point, concavity makes it positive at every subsequent interior point up to1. Thus on this interval rho-rho0 can change sign at most once, from negative to positive.

If a>=a0, the complete difference has at most the sign pattern negative, positive, beginning with the possible model-only left interval. Equal masses then make its first moment strictly positive unless the two densities agree almost everywhere. The actual and reference first moments agree, so this case gives identical densities.

In the nonidentical case a<a0. The actual-only left interval makes rho-rho0 positive there; its complete sign pattern is positive, negative, positive (one of the final intervals may initially appear empty, but the equal zeroth and first moments rule out a nonzero difference with only one sign change). Choose the two crossing points u<=v. The pointwise product

(t-u)(t-v)(rho(t)-rho0(t))

is nonnegative everywhere. Integrating, the zeroth and first moment differences vanish, leaving exactly integral t^2(rho-rho0)>=0. This proves (3.1). If the variances agree, the nonnegative integrand has zero integral, so the two densities agree almost everywhere; a nontrivial continuous difference on an interval would contribute strictly positively. This proves sharpness and the equality description at the marginal-density level. No claim about arbitrary measures satisfying only finitely many moments is used.

## 4. Two elementary sharp consequences and a newly rejected control

The following exact polynomial factorizations prove

5(1-alpha) q^T Sigma q >= alpha,                              (4.1)
q^T Sigma q >= 2alpha/3.                                      (4.2)

Write U=r^2+r+1 and W=3r^2+2r+1. Then

5(1-A)S-A = 3r^4(r^2+2r+3)(r^2+4r+1)/(U^2 W^2),

S-2A/3 = (r-1)^2(6r^6+48r^5+81r^4+72r^3+36r^2+8r+1)/(10U^2 W^2).

Both are nonnegative for all r>=0. The first is sharp at a pyramid r=0, the second at a prism r=1. The simpler inequality (4.2) is stronger than (4.1) when alpha<=7/10. The full exact curve (3.1) is generally stronger than both. These are normal inequalities, not the full matrix (2.3). In covariance-normalized coordinates the facet support distance is at most1/sqrt(S(r)), in particular at most sqrt(3/(2alpha)).

The earlier projected all-apex control is rho2(u,v)=3/13(1+u^2/4) on[-1,1]^2. It passes every exponent-three all-apex/cross-apex test and every positive projectivity using these visible coordinates, but has no uniform convex3D lift. Its u marginal is

rho1(u)=6/13+3u^2/26, -1<=u<=1.

It has mean0, alpha=rho1(1)=15/26 and variance23/65. Therefore

variance-2alpha/3 = -2/65 <0,                                 (4.3)

whereas 5(1-alpha)variance-alpha=29/169>0. Thus the new actual-facet condition (4.2) already rejects this control using its actual endpoint density and second moment. The weaker pyramid-only form (4.1) would not reject it. This is a concrete additional realization constraint beyond all visible-coordinate projective Euler probes. It is NOT a new physical polyhedron or a whole weak-tree exclusion.

## 5. Full matrix1/5 on all homothetic frusta

Consider the actual body with sections ell(z)F, with a fixed2D convex base F of area centroid0 and covariance C_F. A linear translation of section centroids is removable by an affine shear, so oblique homothetic frusta are included. After shifting the actual volume centroid, Sigma is block diagonal with tangent block E[ell(z)^2] C_F and normal entry Var(z). The selected top facet has c=(0,0,h), T=diag(C_F,h^2), alpha=A(r).

The two tangent generalized eigenvalues of B relative to Sigma^-1 are

R_t=4(r^2+r+1)(r^4+r^3+r^2+r+1)/[5(3r^2+2r+1)],

and the normal generalized eigenvalue is R_n=(1-A)S/A. Their exact lower gaps are

R_t-4/5 = 4r^3(r^3+2r^2+3r+3)/[5(3r^2+2r+1)],
R_n-1/5 = 4r^4(r^2+2r+3)(r^2+4r+1)/[5(3r^2+2r+1)^3].

Hence full B>=Sigma^-1/5 holds throughout this actual family. At a centred d-dimensional pyramid with selected base, alpha=d/(d+1) and Sigma=d T/(d+2); the generalized eigenvalues are1/(d+2) in the normal direction and(d+1)/(d+2) tangentially. Consequently no universal coefficient larger than1/5 can hold in3D.

## 6. A reversible actual-boundary slack kernel

This is a separate new cross-branch mechanism, not a proof of full1/5. Work with nu0 above and write q(x)=q_f at a relative-interior point of facet F_f. Intersections have nu0 measure zero. Put K2=E_nu0 xx^T=(d+2)Sigma/d. Divergence gives

E_nu0 x=E_nu0 q=0,
E_nu0 x q^T=I/d,
E_nu0[(a.q(x))xx^T]=0 for every constant vector a,
q(x).x=1.

The endpoint kernel P(x,dy)=(1-q(y).x)nu0(dy) is nonnegative, Markov, stationary for nu0, and generally NOT reversible. Its conditional mean is -x/d, its conditional second moment is K2, and its two-step density is1+q(y).x/d. These statements use the same one actual volume law.

The symmetrized product kernel

R(x,dy)=d/(d+1) (1-q(y).x)(1-q(x).y) nu0(dy)

IS reversible and Markov. Symmetry is literal. Its row integral is d/(d+1)*(1+q(x).x/d)=1. Its conditional mean is

R[y](x)=-(x+d K2 q(x))/(d+1).

The mixed third-looking moment term vanishes by the displayed flux identity. For every b,

<b.x,R(b.x)>_nu0=-2 b^T K2 b/(d+1).

This Rayleigh quotient does not make b.x an eigenfunction in general, since q(x) is not generally linear in x.

For f=s+b.x and any h supported in one facet F, <h,Rh>=0 because the kernel vanishes on F cross F. Positivity of I+R and I-R on L2(nu0), and minimization over that arbitrary h, give the following exact finite facet inequalities, with pi=alpha/d:

pi E_F[2s+d/(d+1)(b.x-b^T K2 q)]^2 <= 2s^2+(d-1)/(d+1)b^T K2 b,       (6.1)

pi E_F[((d+2)b.x+d b^T K2 q)/(d+1)]^2 <= (d+3)/(d+1)b^T K2 b.         (6.2)

For example, expand <f+h,(I+R)(f+h)>; its h-dependent part is ||h||^2+2<h,(I+R)f>. Its minimum is minus the squared norm of (I+R)f restricted to F, proving (6.1). The minus case is identical. These constraints couple actual facet moments to the actual global covariance. Their effective role in complete physical escape packets remains open; no bound on S or U follows simply from their derivation.
