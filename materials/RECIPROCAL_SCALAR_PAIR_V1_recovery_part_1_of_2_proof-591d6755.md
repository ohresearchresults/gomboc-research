## 1. Complete scalar-pair alternative in dimension three
Write A:M=tr(AM). Let symmetric A have inertia(+,-,-), J=adj(A), and let B be a nonzero symmetric3x3 matrix. Exactly one holds:
(I) Some real c>=0 satisfies cJ-B>=0. Then NO M>0 satisfies A:M>=0 and B:M^-1>=0.
(II) Some M>0 satisfies A:M>0 and B:M^-1>0.
The c=0 case is precisely the trivial B<=0,B!=0 exclusion. B=0 is an exception: its weak pair is feasible but its strict pair is not.

### Exclusion proof
For c>0 set C=M^(1/2) A M^(1/2), with eigenvalues a,-b,-d, all a,b,d>0. A:M>=0 gives a>=b+d, hence tr adj(C)=bd-a(b+d)<=-b²-bd-d²<0. Since adj(C)=det(M)M^(-1/2)JM^(-1/2), J:M^-1<0; B:M^-1<=cJ:M^-1<0. For c=0, nonzero negative-semidefinite B has negative pairing with every SPD inverse. Equality A:M=0 is included.

### One-constraint cone fact: self-contained proof
For indefinite J, K={N>=0:J:N<=0} is generated by rank-one vv^T with vJv<=0. Normalize trN=1. A rank-r point,r>=2,is not extreme: symmetric perturbations supported on its range have dimension r(r+1)/2>=3, whereas trD=0 and, when active,J:D=0 impose at most two equations. A nonzero perturbation of either sign is feasible for sufficiently small magnitude. Thus compact-section extreme points have rank1; finite-dimensional convexity gives the claim.
Its dual is K*=PSD+cone(-J). This sum is closed: if S_k-c_kJ converges, evaluating on v with vJv<0 bounds c_k>=0; a subsequence converges and gives the limiting representation. The dual-of-intersection identity, or separation, then proves K*. Therefore [vJv<=0 implies vBv<=0 for all v] iff -B in K* iff B<=cJ for some c>=0.
This is the usual homogeneous one-constraint quadratic alternative, not a novelty claim for that standard fact. Primary background: Argue, Kilinc-Karzan and Wang, Necessary and sufficient conditions for rank-one generated cones, arXiv:2007.07433v2, Section2.5/Lemma12. The present proof is self-contained.

### Constructive converse

M_t=tH+P, M_t^-1=t^-1Hdag+P.
