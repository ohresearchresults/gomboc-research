# ASYMMETRIC_RECIPROCAL_EDGE_HULL_V1

## Statement and scientific scope
Let p>=q>=2. For 1<=u<=q and 1<=v<=p define the normalized reciprocal pair

    A(u,v)=diag(uv,u,1)/(uv+u+1),
    B(u,v)=diag(1,v,uv)/(uv+v+1).

Include every simultaneous real orthogonal conjugation of each pair, and call this compact set R(p,q). Then its exact convex hull is generated by the ONE-PARAMETER boundary family

    conv R(p,q) = conv union_{q<=t<=p} Orb(A(q,t),B(q,t)).        (1)

Here Orb means simultaneous conjugation by the SAME orthogonal matrix. If q>=p>=2, interchange the reciprocal components and the two caps before applying (1).

In contrast, when p!=q the single endpoint orbit Orb(A(q,p),B(q,p)) does NOT generate the whole hull. The failure occurs even for strict interior spectral ratios arbitrarily close to the accepted V10/F9 caps, and even for formal reciprocal pairs satisfying all accepted common-cap inequalities strictly.

Thus the previous two-dimensional asymmetric spectral rectangle reduces exactly to a one-dimensional boundary family, but NOT to a single endpoint. The result concerns a convex relaxation of the common metric and its inverse, not actual realization of every relaxed pair. It proves no whole-polyhedron or H5 exclusion, no stable or unstable census, no new attaining proper110 body, and no improvement of 34<=C_min<=622. No assertion is made here for min(p,q)<2 or for an exact description of the nonconvex interior reciprocal set.

## 1. Exact hull of one endpoint orbit
For arbitrary p,q>1 put P0=pq+q+1 and Q0=pq+p+1. For symmetric trace-one matrices A,B define

    X=[p P0 A+Q0 B-p(q+1)I]/[(p-1)(pq-1)],
    Y=[(pq+1)I-P0 A-Q0 B]/[(p-1)(q-1)],
    Z=[P0 A+q Q0 B-q(p+1)I]/[(q-1)(pq-1)].                   (2)

Direct algebra gives

    trX=trY=trZ=1, X+Y+Z=I,
    A=(pq X+q Y+Z)/P0,
    B=(X+p Y+pq Z)/Q0.                                      (3)

Therefore (A,B) belongs to conv Orb(A(q,p),B(q,p)) if and only if X,Y,Z are all positive semidefinite.

For completeness, the matrix-partition assertion underlying this equivalence has a short real-dimensional proof. Let C be the compact convex set of real symmetric triples X,Y,Z>=0 with traces one and sum I. If all three ranks are at least two, the trace-free symmetric perturbation space supported on each range has dimension at least two. The sum of these three spaces has at least six variables, while the condition that their sum vanish has only five real trace-free symmetric equations. A nonzero supported perturbation therefore preserves the constraints, and sufficiently small perturbations of both signs preserve PSD. Such a triple is not extreme.

If one matrix has rank one, its trace makes it an orthogonal rank-one projector. The other two matrices annihilate that direction and sum to the identity on its two-dimensional orthogonal complement. Unless both are complementary rank-one projectors, they admit a nonzero small opposite trace-free perturbation on that plane. Consequently every extreme triple is an ordered orthogonal rank-one partition of I, and every triple in C is a convex combination of these partitions. Formula (3) takes exactly those extreme partitions to the simultaneous endpoint orbit. This proves the claimed hull description, including noncommuting matrices in its convex hull.

## 2. Why the asymmetric corner is insufficient
Suppose p>q>1, set u=q, and take q<v<p. Substituting the diagonal reciprocal pair A(q,v),B(q,v) into (2) gives

    Z_00 = q(p-v)(q+1)(q-v)
           /[(q-1)(pq-1)(qv+q+1)(qv+v+1)] <0,

    Y_22 = q(p-v)(q-v)
           /[(p-1)(q-1)(qv+q+1)(qv+v+1)] <0.                 (4)

Thus this allowed boundary pair is outside the single endpoint hull. By continuity, u can be decreased slightly below q while keeping both violations strict. The counterexample is therefore not an artefact of using an equality at a spectral cap. The reciprocal interchange gives the corresponding conclusion for q>p.

This refutes only the proposed replacement of the full asymmetric hull by one endpoint orbit. It does not refute any accepted common-cap inequality or any actual polyhedral theorem.

## 3. Fixed-upper-ratio lemma
For p>=q>=2 and every 1<=u<=q, the pair A(u,p),B(u,p) belongs to conv Orb(A(q,p),B(q,p)). By (2), it suffices to prove that its nine scalar diagonal partition entries are nonnegative.

Write a=(up,u,1), b=(1,p,up), P=up+u+1, Q=up+p+1. Clearing the strictly positive entry denominators in (2), the nine numerators are

    NX_j = p P0 a_j Q+Q0 b_j P-p(q+1)P Q,
    NY_j = (pq+1)P Q-P0 a_j Q-Q0 b_j P,
    NZ_j = P0 a_j Q+q Q0 b_j P-q(p+1)P Q.                    (5)

Each has degree at most two in u. Substitute u=1+(q-1)x, 0<=x<=1. For any such numerator N, its degree-two Bernstein coefficients are

    beta0=N(1),
    beta1=N(1)+(q-1)N'(1)/2,
    beta2=N(q).                                             (6)

Equivalently, if N(1+(q-1)x)=c0+c1*x+c2*x^2, the coefficients are c0, c0+c1/2 and c0+c1+c2.

The complete exact source below expands all 27 coefficients after p=q+b and q=2+a. Every expansion has only nonnegative rational coefficients in a,b. Exactly 21 of the 27 coefficient polynomials are nonzero; their expansions contain 362 positive monomials in total. The remaining six are identically zero. The source also checks exact reconstruction of each polynomial from its three Bernstein coefficients, not only their signs. Its report contains every coefficient and every monomial; nothing is sampled or omitted. Since a,b>=0 and the three Bernstein basis functions on [0,1] are nonnegative, all nine numerators in (5) are nonnegative. This proves the lemma.

The restriction q>=2 is part of this proof and of the promoted theorem. Failure of this particular positive-coefficient substitution below q=2 would not itself disprove the lemma there, but no extension is claimed.

## 4. Proof of the boundary-family theorem
Take any pair in R(p,q), with p>=q>=2. If v<=q, both adjacent ratios u,v are at most q. The accepted common-cap theorem puts this pair in the convex hull of the single orbit with spectrum(q^2,q,1), which is the t=q member of the right side of (1).

If q<=v<=p, apply the fixed-upper-ratio lemma with p replaced by the particular value t=v. Since t>=q>=2, it puts A(u,v),B(u,v) in conv Orb(A(q,v),B(q,v)). Simultaneous orthogonal conjugation preserves this inclusion. Thus every generator of the left side lies in the right side.

Conversely, every boundary generator with u=q and q<=t<=p is itself in R(p,q). Convexifying proves equality. The endpoint interval is compact, so this is a closed convex hull in a finite-dimensional space; no singular or limiting metric is substituted for a reciprocal pair.

## 6. Consequence for coupled primal/polar linear cuts
Let H,G be any fixed symmetric objective matrices. For a fixed boundary parameter t in [q,p], put P_t=qt+q+1 and Q_t=qt+t+1. Under (3), minimizing tr(H A)+tr(G B) over the t-endpoint orbit hull is the following exact three-partition SDP, divided by the positive factor P_t Q_t:

    minimize tr(F1 X)+tr(F2 Y)+tr(F3 Z),
    X,Y,Z>=0, trX=trY=trZ=1, X+Y+Z=I,

where

    F1=qt Q_t H+P_t G,
    F2=q Q_t H+t P_t G,
    F3=Q_t H+qt P_t G.                                    (7)

The full asymmetric-hull minimum is the minimum of these values for q<=t<=p, by (1). This is a one-parameter family, not an unjustified evaluation at t=p.

A dual certificate at t uses a symmetric W(t) and scalars z_i(t) with

    F_i(t)-W(t)-z_i(t)I>=0  for i=1,2,3.

It gives the lower bound [tr W(t)+sum_i z_i(t)]/(P_t Q_t). If exact polynomial or rational choices make those PSD conditions and a desired positive numerator hold for every t in [q,p], they give a valid uniform joint linear cut. Multiplying known positive denominators and checking matrix principal minors reduces such a supplied certificate to univariate polynomial inequalities. No such polyhedron-specific cut or tree coverage is constructed in this material.

The two normalized matrices share the same eigenframe and are exact inverses before convexification. Independent choices of their orientation or covariance are not permitted. The convex hull is useful for rigorous necessary cuts, but a surviving relaxed pair need not itself obey A B proportional to I.
