## 1. Actual support replacement, not a misleading intersection
Let K be an actual convex planar support with whole marked edge

    e={t=0,-1<=y<=1}, K subset{t>=0},

and nonnegative affine height ell=a+b t+c y. The uniformly filled3D lift is2|z|<=ell over K. Define

    V=int_K ell, J=int_K t ell, mu=int_K y ell/V,
    Y=y-mu, I=int_K ell Y^2, Bj=int_e ell Y^j dy,
    m=J/V, eta=B1/B2, R=J B2/(V I).

Assume the two endpoints are genuine nondegenerate corners with adjacent straight segments, written near t=0 as

    y_-(t)=-1+v_- t+o(t),
    y_+(t)=1+v_+ t+o(t).

Let H_-=ell(0,-1), H_+=ell(0,1). For any affine psi(y)=p+qY, move the WHOLE marked line to

    t-s psi(y)=0.

For a nonnegative psi on e and positive s, ordinary inward clipping agrees locally with this variation. For sign-changing psi or the opposite s, one must instead replace the old marked boundary, extend its two adjacent straight edges to their new intersections, and keep the rest of the convex boundary. Retaining the old halfplane and merely intersecting would leave pieces of the old facet and would not describe the asserted whole-facet derivative. At genuine corners, the cyclic normal order stays strict for sufficiently small signed s, giving the actual two-sided convex replacement. This assertion is not made for a smooth endpoint, a zero-angle wedge or an arbitrary fixed-incidence class.

If necessary clip the resulting support by ell>=0. Near a density-zero endpoint, the formal versus actual clipped discrepancy is O(s^2) in whole-edge weighted moments and no larger in the required first-order bulk moments. Endpoint flux is multiplied by H=0 there. This is the same actual-affine-density-zero principle as the accepted clipping result, not a licence to omit second-order endpoint corrections in a Hessian. All formulas below concern FIRST order only.

Because partial_t(t-s psi(y))=1, the whole-facet coarea measure in the y coordinate is exactly dy: there is no missing Euclidean slope factor. The raw frozen-old-centre second-moment flux is

    D2[psi]=b int_-1^1 psi(y)Y^2 dy
       +H_+ v_+ psi(1)Y_+^2-H_- v_- psi(-1)Y_-^2.

Both moving endpoints and the affine-height change on the tilted line appear explicitly.

## 2. Differentiating the ACTUAL centre
Put Ej[psi]=int_e ell Y^j psi dy. Since the marked boundary has t=0 and Y is OLD-volume-centred,

    V'=-E0,
    J'=-V p,
    mu'=-E1/V,
    I'=-E2,
    B2'=D2[psi]+2B1 E1/V.

The last term is the actual facet recentering term. Therefore

    (log R)'=-p/m+D2[psi]/B2
       +(1/V)int_e ell psi[1+2eta Y+(V/I)Y^2]dy.

For the pure tilt psi=Y the normal first moment has J'=0 and this specializes to

    D_tilt=D2[Y]/B2+3B1/V+B3/I.

Thus every regular TWO-SIDED stationary whole-facet maximum obeys the exact cubic edge-moment identity

    D2[Y]+3B1B2/V+B2B3/I=0.

The parallel movement psi=1 gives

    D_parallel=D2[1]/B2-1/m+B0/V+2B1^2/(VB2)+B2/I,

which agrees with the previously accepted actual marked-cut formula. The affine nonnegative cone on[-1,1] is generated by psi_-=(1-y)/2 and psi_+=(1+y)/2. Thus a first-order inward-cut maximum needs both endpoint reactions nonpositive; at a regular two-sided maximum both vanish. Their values are

    D[psi_+]=(1+mu)D_parallel/2+D_tilt/2,
    D[psi_-]=(1-mu)D_parallel/2-D_tilt/2.

These identities are algebraic consequences of the same complete actual-moment system. They are not asserted to be logically independent of all density and free-support equations after affine normalization.

At a hypothetical R>3/2 maximum, the accepted alpha=mB0/V<=3/4 bound gives an additional useful sign. With lambda=B1^2/(B0B2)<=1,

    min_Y [1+2eta Y+(V/I)Y^2]
      =1-eta^2 I/V=1-lambda alpha/R>1/2.

The kernel in the edge integral is therefore uniformly positive. It does not by itself determine the sign of endpoint flux or prove the whole inequality.

## 3. Quantitative weighted straight-edge stationarity
Let the same self-consistent parabola be f(y)=A(y^2+2By+C), A>0. Consider any free positive-density straight boundary edge from a marked endpoint (0,y0) to a joining point(f(y1),y1), with L=y1-y0 nonzero. Let x=(y-y0)/L, and H0,H1>=0 be the actual affine heights at its endpoints, H0+H1>0. Writing the joining line as ell_line (not the density),

    ell_line-f=-A L^2(x-1)(x-rho),
    rho=f(y0)/(A L^2).

A free rotation about the fixed marked endpoint has normal velocity proportional to x. Its weighted first-variation equation is precisely

    int_0^1 x(x-1)(x-rho)[H0+(H1-H0)x]dx=0.

Direct integration gives the numerator

    [5H0 rho-2H0+5H1 rho-3H1]/60.

Consequently every such stationary edge has

    rho=(2H0+3H1)/[5(H0+H1)] in[2/5,3/5].

For strictly positive endpoint heights the interval is strict. A whole zero-density edge H0=H1=0 has no such condition and is excluded from this statement. Equal endpoint heights recover the uniform-density fraction1/2.

Moreover

    f'(y1)-line_slope=A L(1-rho).

The magnitude of the derivative jump is between(2/5)A|L| and(3/5)A|L|. This is a quantitative transversality conclusion, not merely non-tangency. It applies to an actually free signed edge rotation; it does not assert that every constrained corner is free or stationary.

## 4. Two exact valid one-arc geometric controls invalidate a sign shortcut
A natural shortcut would try to combine BOTH weighted straight-edge reactions with convexity and deduce a fixed sign for

    Ftrial=int_K t(t-f(y)) dt dy.

At an actual self-consistent maximum, the additional affine-density direction psi=t requires this expression to vanish. But the two line reactions alone do NOT give it a definite sign, even in strictly positive-density one-arc bodies. The following controls prove that specific failure.

Use d>0,0<z<1, two fractions ru,rl in(2/5,3/5), and

    B=[d^2 z^2 rl-(d+2)^2 ru]/4,
    C=[(d+2)^2 ru+d^2 z^2 rl]/2-1,
    A=1/[(1+d)^2+2B(1+d)+C],
    f=A(y^2+2By+C), u=1+dz, bfar=1+d,
    L=f(u)/z, U=(y+1)/(d+2), S=L(y-1)/d.

The support's upper graph is U on[-1,bfar]. Its lower graph is0 on[-1,1], S on[1,u], f on[u,bfar]. Put

    RU=(5ru-2)/(3-5ru), RL=(5rl-2)/(3-5rl),
    Delta=(u-RL)-Lz(bfar+RU),
    k=[RL-1-Lz(RU-1)]/Delta,
    beta=RU-1-(bfar+RU)k,
    ell=1+k y+beta t.

These expressions enforce the two endpoint-height fractions exactly. Delta=0 needs separate analysis in a general parametrization; no division by it is made without checking in these controls.
