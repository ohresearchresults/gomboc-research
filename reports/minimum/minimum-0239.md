## 4. Every normal-blind spoke EF has an exact tangential inverse identity

Let r->s be a physical spoke EF with gamma=0. Write r=(r_H,r_z), s-r=(d_H,d_z), with horizontal components in R^2. Let b be the spoke's F endpoint. The bearing facet R has a true F-boundary edge from b to another basal vertex b'; put

    e=b'-b,       e_z=0.

Because R contains that edge, r_H.e_H=0. The source plane is lateral, so r_H!=0. Since Q_rs=0 and r_z d_z=0,

    r_H.d_H=0.

Moreover the adjacent facet S does not contain b', by true face intersection. Therefore

    d_H.e_H=(s-r).(b'-b)<0.

Both e_H and d_H lie in the one-dimensional perpendicular space to r_H. Hence

    d_H=-c e_H,       c>0.                              (4.1)

This argument covers r_z=0, d_z=0, and their intersection. In particular d_H cannot vanish.

Now keep the actual body fixed and use ANY block-diagonal physical pair

    M(G,lambda)=diag(G,lambda),
    N(G,lambda)=diag(G^-1,1/lambda),
    G positive definite, lambda>0.                      (4.2)

For a symmetric two-by-two matrix, adj(G)=(tr G)I-G. Using r_H.e_H=0 and r_z d_z=0 gives the EXACT identity

    Q_rs(G,lambda)
      =-c r_H^T G^-1 e_H
      = c r_H^T G e_H/det G.                            (4.3)

There is no centroid approximation: the body is unchanged and still uniformly centred. There is no independent inverse: the determinant in (4.3) comes from that same G. The normal parameter disappears identically, at all orders.

Thus each blind EF contributes the explicit tangential request

    r_H^T H e_H>0                                       (4.4)

for G=I+tH. Because r_H.e_H=0, the trace part of H is irrelevant. Write

    H(u,v)=[[u,v],[v,-u]],

            a_x e_y+a_y e_x).                           (4.5)

Then a^T H e=w(a,e).(u,v). All requests below are therefore two-dimensional geometric vectors, not arbitrary four-column mechanical tensors.

2. If there is any non-carried cross contact other than a spoke EF—namely a spoke VE, lateral VF at an end, or lateral-bearing EF on an end boundary—then sigma=+1.

       y_i^T H e_ij >=0   for every end-carried VE,

## 6. Equal-height blind poles force actual end contacts or a strict U

There is a stronger physical interpretation when the blind EF satisfies r_z=s_z, not merely r_z=0.

Let a be its T endpoint and b its F endpoint. Since d_z=0, (s-r).a=(s-r).b=0 puts a_H,b_H in the line spanned by r_H. Write R^2=|r_H|^2, rho=|r|^2 and z_p=r_z/rho. The foot lies in the spoke interior, so k<z_p<h. Actual incidence gives

    a=alpha r+theta q_T,
    alpha=(1-k r_z)/R^2,
    theta=k rho(k-z_p)/R^2>0;

    b=beta r+psi q_F,
    beta=(1-h r_z)/R^2,
    psi=h rho(h-z_p)/R^2>0.                             (6.1)

If r_z<=0, then beta>0. Hence b is a genuine VE on the true edge F cap R. The positive normal combination in (6.1) lies in the relative interior of that edge's normal cone.

If r_z>=0, then alpha>0. If R is a quad, a is a genuine VE on T cap R. If R is a lateral triangle, q_T and r are nonadjacent vertices of the polar facet corresponding to a. Their strict convex combination lies in its relative interior, so a is a **strict unstable vertex** of the original body. This is not a weak contact or an algebraic zero being counted as U.

Whenever the forced end VE exists, its exact tangential row is a positive multiple of r_H^T G e_R. Thus (4.3) shows that its successful tangential exit also resolves the separate blind EF, with a positive factor for every G>0 and lambda>0. No additional tangential selector is needed for that EF.

For weak S1U1 this has a concrete consequence: equal-height blind spoke EFs with r_z>=0 borne by lateral triangles must all have the SAME top endpoint, namely the unique strict U. Two different such endpoints would already contradict U=1. Equal-height blind contacts with r_z<=0 necessarily engage a basal weak vertex, explaining why a top-only support-plane construction cannot simply ignore their interference with F.

The case r_z=0 but s_z!=r_z still has the exact inverse identity (4.3), but the endpoint decomposition (6.1) is not asserted there. These two normal-blind mechanisms must not be conflated.

## 7. A top-fixed support-plane construction for arbitrary spoke-EF signs

The following construction bypasses the normal pencil entirely. It is useful in the genuinely mixed-sign case left by Theorems 3 and 5.

Label the seven lateral poles r_i cyclically, and put q_0=q_T. For constants k_i prescribe

    r_i(t)=q_0+(1+t k_i)(r_i-q_0).                       (7.1)

Every top vertex a incident to r_i satisfies a.q_0=a.r_i=1; therefore a.r_i(t)=1 exactly. All three top vertices and their entire triangle stay pointwise fixed. The factors 1+t k_i are positive locally, including when k_i<0.

The seven base vertices are simple. With q_F temporarily variable, each is the intersection of q_F and its two adjacent lateral planes. Thus these pole prescriptions define a genuine same-lattice realization locally. The only remaining task is to keep the actual uniform centroid at zero.

### 7.1 Actual-centroid completion by F

For a moving supporting pole p of a true facet, let

    w=Area(F_p)/|p|,
    T_p=mean_(F_p) xx^T,
    V=Vol(P).

At actual centroid zero, the derivative of the uniform centroid is

    D_p g=-(w/V)T_p.                                    (7.2)

This follows from the actual boundary normal velocity -(delta p.x)/|p| and differentiation of the volume first moment. The matrix T_p is SPD: a nonzero linear form cannot vanish on an open subset of an affine facet plane not through zero. The inherited whole-facet derivative agrees with (7.2). fileciteturn28file0L20-L23

Since every F vertex is simple, its entire supporting plane can move in three parameters. Equation (7.2) is invertible. The analytic implicit function theorem therefore gives a unique nearby pole q_F(t) keeping the ORIGINAL uniformly filled body's actual centroid zero, while preserving all prescribed r_i(t) and q_0. All moments are computed from the genuine oriented fan, not from freely chosen masses.

In particular the final top coordinates and lateral poles are exactly the prescribed ones. The centroid completion does not add an uncomputed correction to a lateral-lateral Q row.

### 7.2 Exact response at a spoke EF

For an original physical EF r_i->r_j put

    rho_i=r_i^T N r_i,
    A_i=rho_i-q_0^T N r_i>0,
    B_i=A_i+q_0^T N(r_j-r_i).                            (7.3)

Positivity of A_i follows from A_i=rho_i(1-q_0.p_i) and p_i lying in the open spoke, not on T. Differentiating the true prescribed poles in (7.1), with fixed M,N, gives

    Q_ij'=A_i k_j-B_i k_i.                              (7.4)

Also

    rho_j-rho_i=|r_j-r_i|_N^2>0.                         (7.5)

The directed graph D of physical spoke-EF arrows is therefore acyclic. Each lateral facet has at most one such outgoing arrow because its perpendicular foot is unique. D is a rooted forest contained in the seven-spoke lateral cycle.

There is ALWAYS a solution of (7.4)>0 if all k_i are free. In increasing order of rho, use for example

    k_j=1+sum_(i->j in D) max(0,(B_i/A_i)k_i).            (7.6)

Every k_j is positive and strictly exceeds each required lower bound. This is a deterministic actual-pole construction; the B_i may have either sign and gamma does not enter its feasibility.

### Theorem 7.1: arbitrary-sign spoke EFs plus the complete top carrier

If the entire weak packet consists of contacts carried by T together with interior-spoke EFs, then it regularizes analytically with fixed M,N. Use (7.1), (7.6) and the actual F-centroid completion. All spoke EFs open at order one. All top-carried contacts remain exactly neutral: top vertex coordinates are fixed, and

    q_0^T N(r_i(t)-q_0)
      =(1+t k_i)q_0^T N(r_i-q_0).                        (7.7)

Then apply the ordinary top-carrier opening at amplitude t^2. Its actual shape and centroid perturbations are O(t^2), too late to change the positive spoke-EF first terms.

For an explicit top-carrier prescription, put p_T=Nq_0/(q_0^T Nq_0), y_i=a_i-p_T, R_i=y_i^T M y_i and prescribe

    a_i(eta)=a_i+eta(K R_i y_i+b),       eta=t^2.          (7.8)

Take b=0, an inward translation at a bearing EF, or an outgoing edge at a bearing VF. The inherited positive K bound makes all carried VE exits positive. At a VF this supplies a genuine complementary pair, including a right corner.

The extension can be made jointly analytic: for each quad solve its two moving top incidences with one fixed auxiliary linear normalization; for each lateral triangle solve its one moving top incidence with two fixed auxiliary normalizations. Thus (7.8) is a real same-lattice construction through the moving body, not merely prescribed velocities. Original nonzero guards remain open.

### 7.3 Additional top-located contacts

This construction can also retain weak points on T which are not carried by T. Mark a set Z of lateral poles as follows: mark the bearing pole of every lateral-bearing EF on a T edge and every lateral-bearing VF at a T vertex; for a VE at a T vertex on a spoke, mark BOTH incident lateral poles.

If k_i<0 at every marked node, all these extra top-located contacts have positive first-order exits. For a top VE at a with spoke endpoint b, write Ma=alpha r_i+beta r_j with alpha,beta>0. Since a stays fixed,

    P_ab'=-(1-q_0.b)(alpha k_i+beta k_j)>0.              (7.9)

For a lateral-bearing EF on a T edge,

    Q_(r_i,q_0)'=-k_i |q_0-r_i|_N^2>0.                 (7.10)

At a lateral-bearing VF, Ma=r_i/rho_i. Every bearing spoke edge has derivative

    P_ab'=-k_i(1-q_0.b)/rho_i>0.                         (7.11)

If the bearing facet is a quad, the complementary dual exit across its T edge is positive by (7.10). If it is a triangle, both primal exits in (7.11) are positive. In its true polar corner q_0-r_i is a positive combination of the two corner-edge directions, and q_0^T N(q_0-r_i)=|q_0-r_i|_N^2>0. At least one of the corresponding dual derivatives -k_i q_0^T N(s-r_i) is positive. Choose its complementary primal exit. This checks the VF pair rather than just one side of it.

Let A be all ancestors of Z in D, including Z. If B_i>0 on every arrow i->j whose target lies in A, assign k_i=-a_i<0 on A in reverse norm order by

    a_i=1+(A_i/B_i)a_j  when i->j stays in A,
    a_i=1              otherwise.

Assign positive values outside A in forward norm order using (7.6), now including incoming negative values from A. There is no arrow from outside A into A. Equations (7.4) and (7.9)–(7.11) are all strictly positive.

Accordingly, the whole packet regularizes whenever all weak locations lie on T or in spoke interiors and this stated ancestral sign condition holds. ## 8. Two lateral triangles always provide full actual-COM correction

The preceding construction used F to repair the centroid, and therefore did not protect an arbitrary weak packet on F. The following actual-moment lemma supplies a different repair.

### Lemma 8.1

At every actual-centred parallel heptagon–triangle body, any TWO distinct lateral triangular facets provide a rank-three uniform-centroid actuator if each pole may vary in the two-dimensional plane preserving its incident top vertex. F, T, all top vertices and every other supporting plane can be held fixed.

### Proof

Consider a lateral triangle G=[a,b,c], with a in T and b,c in F, and normalized pole r. Put theta=k/h and let

    T_G=mean_G xx^T
       =[(a+b+c)(a+b+c)^T+aa^T+bb^T+cc^T]/12.

Then

    T_G r=(a+b+c)/3,
    T_G q_F=[(2theta+2)a+(theta+3)(b+c)]/12,

so

    T_G^-1 a
      =12/(theta-1) [q_F-(theta+3)r/4].                 (8.1)

Allowed pole variations satisfy a.delta r=0. By (7.2), their centroid image is a two-dimensional plane with normal T_G^-1 a.

The actual uniform centroid satisfies theta>-3. Indeed, cone a true boundary triangulation to a top vertex at height k. Every nonzero tetrahedron has mean height at most (k+3h)/4. Equality for the entire body would force all volume outside that apex to lie in the plane z=h, making it a pyramid with only one extreme vertex at z=k. The true triangular end has three vertices and positive area, so the inequality is strict. Since the actual mean height is zero, k+3h>0.

Consequently c_0=(theta+3)/4 is nonzero. For two distinct lateral triangular poles r_1,r_2, the vectors q_F-c_0 r_1 and q_F-c_0 r_2 cannot be parallel: otherwise the three distinct true polar vertices q_F,r_1,r_2 would be collinear. Their two centroid image planes are therefore different and span R^3.

All plane changes are genuine. Each moving triangle keeps its nonsimple top vertex by the exact incidence a.r=1; its other two vertices are simple intersections on F. Simultaneous changes, even in triangles sharing a top or basal vertex, preserve the labelled incidence locally. No independent movement of fan triangles is involved.

Select the first nonsingular three-column minor among the four allowed pole coordinates, hold the fourth coordinate fixed, and apply the analytic implicit function theorem to g=0. This corrects any sufficiently small prescribed changes of the other supporting planes, while holding the two end planes and their top vertices fixed.

This proof uses the actual uniform centroid in an essential way. At the formal reference-height value theta=-3 the normals in (8.1) would all be proportional to q_F, and this two-triangle rank argument would collapse. That value is excluded here by real uniform-volume geometry, not by a metric choice.

## 9. Full-packet isolation theorem for mixed adverse and blind spokes

This theorem allows weak contacts on BOTH ends. Its hypotheses specify exactly where the actual centroid correction and the primary spoke motions may act.

### 9.1 The protected lateral-pole footprint

Build a set H of lateral poles from the original physical weak packet, omitting the spoke-EF groups themselves.

For every originally zero outgoing P row at an original weak vertex, except a row whose two endpoints are in T, include in H the two lateral poles incident to each basal endpoint of that row. For every originally zero outgoing Q row at an original weak facet other than T, include each lateral pole occurring in that row. At VF include the full two-primal/two-dual zero groups, not an arbitrarily compressed higher-order row.

With both end planes fixed, keeping H fixed keeps all these P and Q values EXACTLY fixed. Basal vertices are intersections of F and their two lateral poles; top vertices are fixed separately. The omitted within-T P values are automatically fixed. The omitted T-source Q values retain the positive factor (7.7). Thus this footprint definition is an explicit isolation proof.

Assume there are two distinct lateral triangles G_1,G_2 which belong neither to H nor to the set of endpoint poles of D. These two planes will be used only for the centroid correction of Lemma 8.1. They do not occur in any spoke-EF row. They cannot be neighbors of T, because T's lateral neighbors are the three quads, so their corrective motions do not spoil (7.7).

### 9.2 An exact directed test with some poles fixed

Set k_i=0 for i in H, and allow all other D-node rates to vary. Since A_i>0, the spoke requirements are

    A_i k_j-B_i k_i>0,       i->j in D.                  (9.1)

For this physical rooted forest there is a simple complete test for the rates in (9.1). It is more explicit than a generic cone solver.

Start a set R of forced-positive nodes with every target of an arrow whose source is fixed, and every target of an arrow with B_i=0. Repeatedly add j whenever i is in R and i->j has B_i>0. Then
