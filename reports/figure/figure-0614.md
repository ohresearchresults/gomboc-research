## 2. Exact all-height actual-mass obstruction

### General theorem

Let B be a bounded full-dimensional convex polyhedron invariant under R. Suppose `a=(0,0,b)` is its only axis vertex and is its unique lowest-z point. Let v be an adjacent non-axis vertex. Define

```text
Delta = v_z-b > 0
rho2 = v_x^2+v_y^2
k = rho2/Delta^2
D = rho2+Delta*(v_z+b-2*g_B,z).
```

If **k>1/2 and D>0**, then, for **every real h>b**, the homogeneous body

`B_h = B intersect {z<=h}`

has **at least three strictly unstable equilibria at its actual uniform centroid**. There is no restriction to small centre displacement, no assumption that S1 persists, and no finite upper bound on h. Other equilibria may be weak; the three asserted U are strict.

**Proof.** Half-turn symmetry places the actual centroid c_h on the axis. The unique lowest point a is strictly unstable. For b<h<=v_z, the edge a--v supplies

`p_h=a+(h-b)*(v-a)/Delta`.

Coning a triangulated boundary to a shows the universal centroid inequality `c_h,z<=(b+3h)/4`: every positive-volume tetrahedron has one vertex at a and three vertices at heights at most h. Consequently,

```text
|p_h-c_h|^2-|a-c_h|^2
  = (k+1)*(h-b)^2+2*(h-b)*(b-c_h,z)
 >= (k-1/2)*(h-b)^2 > 0.
```

For h>=v_z, v remains in the body and removal of an upper tail cannot increase the scalar mean, so c_h,z<=g_B,z. The squared-radius difference at v is therefore at least D>0. Thus a is never globally farthest.

A globally farthest vertex u is always strict U, since for every distinct body vertex y,

`2*(u-c)·(y-u)=|y-c|²-|u-c|²-|y-u|²<0`.

The new cut cannot create an axis vertex other than a: at an intermediate height the axis point lies in the relative interior of the two-dimensional section; the unchanged body has no other axis vertex. Hence a farthest vertex is off axis, and its distinct half-turn partner is also a strict farthest U. Together with a this proves U>=3. **The proof covers support changes along the entire height family.**
