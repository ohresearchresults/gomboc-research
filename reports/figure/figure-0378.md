## 5. A reusable mass mechanism and its missing freedom

The following derivations are self-contained and do not depend on the omitted body capsule.

For a nonzero owner q and p=q/2+v,

    p·(q−p) = |q|²/4−|v|².

Therefore the open diametral ball |v|<|q|/2 parametrizes a strict positive escape p→q. An acyclic owner forest reaching one root provides a full unstable-exclusion mechanism, provided all witnesses are tested about the same centre and the root’s strict cone is checked separately. Owners need not be adjacent; a positive dot product with any other body point excludes an unstable equilibrium.

Actual mass closure is an additional equation. If an entire positive-volume body lies in the one-level ball B(q/2,|q|/2), then q·x>=|x|²>=0 throughout it, with strict positivity on positive volume. Hence **q·g>0**. Such a one-level star cannot have uniform volume centroid0. This identifies a missing material degree of freedom in that parametrization; it does not exclude a multilevel forest or arbitrary geometry.

For m successive backward diametral-ball steps from a vector of length R, the support capacity in a unit direction at angle beta to that vector is

    sup n·p_m = R·cos(beta/(m+1))^(m+1),  m>=1.

For a positive-projection chain, multiply the radial cosine bounds for its angular steps and the final projection cosine. The spherical triangle inequality makes the total angle at least beta; concavity of log(cos) bounds the product by equal angular steps. Equal great-circle steps with maximal closed-ball radial factors attain the bound; strict balls approach it. In the opposite direction the capacities are0,R/8,R/4 for one,two,three steps. Thus depth supplies genuine counter-material freedom absent from a direct owner star, but does not by itself solve simultaneous monostability.
