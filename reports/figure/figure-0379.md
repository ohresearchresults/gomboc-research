## 5. A whole-body obstruction about the actual centroid

Let B be any full-dimensional convex polyhedron already translated to its own centroid0, with radial function R(v) on the unit sphere.

**Two unstable equilibria.** Suppose a central plane cuts B in a polygon whose maximum squared radius is W, and B has a point in each open halfspace with squared radius greater than W. On each closed hemisphere, R achieves a maximum away from its equator. Such a local radial maximum on a convex polyhedron is a vertex. Every incident edge derivative must be strictly negative: a zero derivative would still give a positive quadratic increase along that edge. Thus the two halfspaces contain distinct strict U.

**Two stable equilibria.** Suppose a central plane section has minimum boundary squared radius B0 and there is a radial exit in each hemisphere with squared radius below B0. Each hemisphere has an interior local radial minimum. It cannot occur at a vertex: the inward tangent cone contains the direction towards the interior origin, which forces a decreasing incident-edge direction. It cannot occur in the interior of a true edge either: writing its radius vector in the two outward normals, the two inward face derivatives required for a minimum would force both normal coefficients nonpositive, contradicting positive support offsets and positive squared radius. Hence each minimum lies strictly inside a maximal facet, giving two strict S.

These statements require neither the same old equilibrium carriers nor a preserved contact graph. They do require the section/witness inequalities to hold **at the changed body’s actual centre**.

For both exact endpoints, the U certificate uses plane y=0 and the S certificate plane z=0. The program intersects every true edge with the plane, reconstructs the entire exact section polygon, maximizes squared norm at its vertices, minimizes squared norm over every section-boundary segment, and computes the two ±z ray exits from all actual supporting planes.
