## 3. Complete-chord radial-barcode theorem

### Statement and proof

Let0 lie inside full-dimensional convex polyhedron K. Let X contain all vertices and optionally additional points in K. For distinct u,v put w(u,v)=min over t in[0,1] of `|(1-t)u+tv|²`. With A=u.u, B=u.v, C=v.v, D=A+C-2B>0:

```text
w(u,v) = A                         if B>=A;
         C                         if B>=C;
         (A*C-B*B)/D                otherwise.
```

At each level h>=0 retain nodes of squared radius>=h and edges of w>=h. The complete graph on X and true maximal edge graph on K's vertices have identical connected-component persistence, apart from zero-length classes introduced by redundant points. Their strictly positive `(birth,death)` multisets therefore agree.

A retained chord lies outside the open ball of squared radius h. Radial projection from0 onto K's boundary is continuous along it, fixes boundary endpoints and cannot decrease radius because the chord lies in K. It produces an outside boundary path.

The boundary's true one-skeleton captures these components. For a maximal convex polygon F missing the open ball, its entire boundary is available. Otherwise choose a in relint(F) inside the open ball. Push an outside point along its ray from a to the polygon boundary. Convexity of the ball prevents re-entry when extending beyond an outside point; the continuous push fixes outside boundary points. A boundary path can therefore be moved face by face onto actual polygon edges without entering the open ball. A radial chord image is finite piecewise algebraic, so these facewise replacements suffice; equivalently the connectivity relations glue over the finite complex.

Every complete-chord connection thus exists in the true-edge graph. The converse is immediate. These identifications commute as h varies, proving zero-dimensional persistence equality, including exact merger levels.

For an added nonvertex p, write p as a convex combination of genuine vertices. The corresponding weighted sum of `p.(v-p)` is zero, so some genuine vertex v satisfies `p.(v-p)>=0`. Chord p--v never drops below|p|². Thus p attaches at its own birth and adds no positive bar. Remove duplicated points before applying the distinct-pair formula; the origin only attaches at level0.
