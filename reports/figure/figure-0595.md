### What the area barrier fixes, and what it does not

For an actual polar point q_f, use `W_f=max_j q_f.(q_j-q_f)/|q_f|^2`, with physical facet area A_f. The new barrier weights are `100 A_f/sum A`. When a strictly nonstable face splits into pieces with polar points tending to the same q, W tends to the same positive value and the piece areas add. Its weighted barrier therefore has the same limit as the unsplit face. An unweighted sum instead depends on how many pieces appear.

This continuity does **not** cover a split stable carrier. The old root face was omitted from the nonroot barrier. After a split, nonroot pieces of positive limiting area have W tending to0. The unclipped logarithmic penalty diverges; the actually implemented softened barrier instead contributes a nonzero limiting penalty and can create a positive jump relative to the omitted parent. Area weighting is therefore a numerical search device, not an equilibrium certificate, and it can still obstruct an otherwise admissible chart.
