## Why the strip still transfers instead of cancels

The thirteen-edge obstruction is genuinely bypassed: both main branches are fourteen-edge strict dominance chains. The failure has moved to the two-dimensional coupling.

Exact centroid closure requires the counterbranch to carry a large opposing first moment. Its source row `29` becomes the extra stable face, and its attachment to the protected basin creates saddle `(17,29)`. The triangulated communication rungs then leave three additional unstable-saddle transfers: the unstable incidences `(2,3,17)`, `(4,5,18)`, and `(6,36,37)` are paired with saddle edges `(2,16)`, `(4,18)`, and the neighboring cross-strip cone; the robust target-side unstable vertex `(22,33,34)` remains. Suppressing a rung merely transfers its index to the adjacent rung or restores the counterbranch source. This is why metric polishing descends from `(1,11,10)` to `(2,4,4)` but does not enter `(1,1,0)`.

The smallest surviving connected obstruction is therefore not a too-short chain. It is the counterbranch stable source `row 29` plus the four-rung U–H ladder supported on rows around `{0,2,3,4,16,17,18,22,29,33,34,36,37,44}`.
