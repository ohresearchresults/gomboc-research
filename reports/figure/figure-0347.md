## New mechanism: alternating rational incidence ownership

The useful change is to regard **both support vectors and exposed physical contact points as movable reconstruction nodes**. The old support-only chart can become blocked when one support belongs to four or five higher-order contacts: solving that support from three fixed contact planes freezes the others or creates a dependency cycle. The new chart moves some physical-point coordinates from already reconstructed supports instead.

Take the bipartite graph whose nodes are q_i and the designated P_j, with an edge for q_i dot P_j=1. Peel a nonroot node of remaining degree at most three; reverse that order to reconstruct. Each record then needs at most a three-variable linear solve. This permits four- and five-contact supports without imposing four independent equations on one isolated three-vector.

There is a local geometric reason this construction order is available for a genuine convex endpoint. Its vertex-facet incidence graph is simple, planar and bipartite; the designated-contact graph is a subgraph. For n>=3, the planar bipartite bound m<=2n-4 contradicts every nonroot node having degree at least four, even allowing the fixed root arbitrary degree. Smaller remaining graphs are immediate. At a genuine maximal polygon, any three distinct extreme vertices are noncollinear; their plane has equation q dot P=1 and does not pass through the origin, so the corresponding three vectors are linearly independent.
