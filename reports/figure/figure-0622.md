## 5. A finite convex projective mass-closure chart

This provides an executed alternative to unconstrained fitting of a reference centre. It does not preserve equilibria automatically.

Let K be a full-dimensional convex body containing0 in its interior. On

`D={a:1+a·x>0 for every x in K}`,

define `T_a(x)=x/(1+a·x)`. Its Jacobian determinant is `(1+a·x)^-4`. Therefore

`V(a)=Vol(T_a K)=integral_K (1+a·x)^-4 dx`,

`gradient V(a)=-4 integral_K x(1+a·x)^-5 dx=-4 M(T_a K)`,

and

`Hessian V(a)=20 integral_K xx^T(1+a·x)^-6 dx`.

The Hessian is strictly positive definite. The parameter domain is bounded because K contains a ball about0. For a polyhedron, triangulating its boundary into positive origin tetrahedra gives

`V(a)=sum det(x_i,x_j,x_k)/(6(1+a·x_i)(1+a·x_j)(1+a·x_k))`.

Every term is positive. Approaching the domain boundary makes a denominator incident to a vertex tend to zero, so V tends to infinity. Hence V has a unique interior minimizer, and **that finite projectivity makes the actual homogeneous first moment exactly zero**. This is a global existence/uniqueness statement for mass closure, not for110. The minimizer need not be rational.

For normalized H rows `n_i·x<=1`, this transformation is simply the common shift

`n_i -> n_i+a`.

It is a genuine change of the physical hull, preserving its face lattice while the projectivity remains finite. It is not transport of the old homogeneous centroid.

At a=0, writing J for the raw second moment matrix and g=M/V,

`-dg/da = B = 5J/V - 4gg^T`,

which is positive definite. At a centred body it is five times the covariance matrix. Let C be the derivative of the actual centroid with respect to all active normal coordinates and U the matrix repeating the3×3 identity for a common shift. Then `CU=-B`.
