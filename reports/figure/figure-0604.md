## 2. Definite result: a complete depth-three primal forest cannot have its reference point as its homogeneous centroid

### Statement

Let K be a full-dimensional convex polyhedron, represented in coordinates relative to a proposed reference point0. Let r be one of its vertices, with R=|r|>0. Suppose that **every vertex** x of K can reach r in at most m radial-chord steps

`x=x0, x1, …, xk=r`, with k≤m,

satisfying

`xi·(x(i+1)−xi) ≥ 0`.

The intermediate vectors may be chosen independently for different starting vertices. For this necessary condition they may even be virtual points outside K. Thus the theorem does not depend on one fixed tree or on current true-edge adjacency.

For m≤3, the actual uniform-volume centroid g of K satisfies

`r·g / |r|² ≥ 1/16`.

Consequently **g cannot equal0**. In particular, no homogeneous own-centred body can satisfy the stated complete depth-three one-root condition, regardless of its edge count, its stable count, or the number of available relay vertices.

There is also the entirely rational scale-free consequence

`|g| / V^(1/3) ≥ 1/32`,

where V is the actual filled volume. Therefore a normalized mass objective

`(1/2)|g|² / V^(2/3)`

is at least `1/2048` throughout this family. Uniform shrinking cannot evade this bound.

### Radial-envelope proof

A nonzero radial-chord step implies

`|xi| ≤ |x(i+1)| cos(theta_i)`,

where theta_i is the angle between consecutive vectors. Thus each step angle is less thanπ/2, and the root has norm at least that of every starting vertex.

Put u=r/R. To bound a vertex’s backward projection, consider only x with `−u·x>0`, and let alpha be the angle from−u to x. The triangle inequality for spherical angles gives

`alpha + theta_0 + … + theta_(k−1) ≥ π`.

Multiplying the step inequalities and including the projection factor gives

`−u·x ≤ R cos(alpha) product_i cos(theta_i)`.

Concavity of log(cos) on the relevant interval yields

`−u·x ≤ R cos(π/(k+1))^(k+1)`.

Padding a shorter nonnegative route with repeated root vectors is permissible for this upper bound. Therefore the complete depth-m hypothesis gives

`h_K(−u) ≤ R c_m`,

with

```text
c1 = 0
c2 = 1/8
c3 = 1/4.
```

The maximum forward projection is exactlyR: all vertices have norm at mostR and r itself belongs to K.

### Homogeneous-centroid proof

For any full-dimensional convex three-dimensional body whose projection onto a unit axis lies in[−d,b], its uniform centroid coordinate mu satisfies

`mu ≥ (b−3d)/4`.

Here is a direct proof. Choose a highest point a, with axis coordinate b. For0≤t≤1, the homothetic copy `a+t(K−a)` lies inside K, has volume t³V, and lies above height `b−t(b+d)`. If X is uniformly distributed in K and T=b−u·X, then

`Pr(T≤t(b+d)) ≥ t³`.

Integrating the complementary tail gives `E[T]≤3(b+d)/4`, proving the inequality.

Apply this with b=R and d≤R/4. It follows that

`u·g ≥ R/16`.

This uses the **actual homogeneous mass of K**. It does not use a centroid assigned to its polar, a density transport, or a selected reference census.

Finally K lies inside the Euclidean radius-R ball and hence inside a cube of volume8R³. Thus `V^(1/3)≤2R`, and

`|g|/V^(1/3) ≥ (R/16)/(2R)=1/32`.

### Coupled affine version

For a positive-definite physical metric G=AᵀA, apply the same argument to the homogeneous affine image AK. If the complete routes satisfy

`xiᵀG(x(i+1)−xi) ≥ 0`

and have depth at most three, then

`rᵀGg / (rᵀGr) ≥ 1/16`.

The centroid relation is the genuine affine identity `g(AK)=A g(K)`. This statement never treats the physical and polar metrics as independent forms.

### Exact scope

This is a **complete-forest depth obstruction**, not a bound on the number of newly added points. Existing vertices can supply many links. It does not apply merely because the final newly added bridge has two or three links: an endpoint that reaches the elder through an older, longer path can make the complete route substantially deeper.

It also does not exclude forests of depth four or greater, a succession of different witness policies, boundary changes that introduce longer routes, or the QW49D family itself. I have not established the depth-three hypothesis for the attempted new numerical continuations. In particular, the current short-port idea is not ruled out just because its new segment is short.
