## 3. Whole-shape mechanism: a protected ascent structure with material inside it

### 3.1 A finite point-bank certificate for one unstable equilibrium

Let a spine consist of physical points

`u0,…,um`, with `um=(1,0,0)`,

satisfying

`ui·(u(i+1)−ui)>0` for every i<m.

A point x satisfying

`x·(u−x)>0`

lies strictly inside the ball with diameter[0,u]. This is the exact identity

`|x−u/2|²<|u|²/4`.

It gives an ascent witness from x to u. Such witnesses compose: every point in a finite bank can be assigned a strictly increasing-radius route to the apex, provided every indicated dot product is positive.

Now form the **actual convex hull** of the complete bank. Any actual vertex other than the apex still has its witness point in the hull, even when the witness link is not itself an exposed edge. A positive dot product against a point of the hull excludes that vertex from being an unstable equilibrium. The apex, being uniquely farthest, is a strict U. Thus the hull has exactly one unstable equilibrium at the specified centre.

This is a certificate about all potential extreme points, not a claim that every routing link is a true exposed edge. The true-edge count is independently reconstructed afterward.

### 3.2 Paired angular material

A useful physical pairing is the half-turn

`R(x,y,z)=(x,−y,−z)`.

For a spine point ui, introduce a paired shadow point

`vi(s)=(ui,x,ui,y/2,ui,z/2+ai+sbi)`

and its half-turn image. The shadow-to-spine exit is exactly

`vi(s)·(ui−vi(s))=(ui,y²+ui,z²)/4−(ai+sbi)²`.

Consequently, a strict bound on the two endpoint values of |ai+sbi| proves the ascent inequality on the entire closed interval0≤s≤1. The shadow points carry real three-dimensional material through their convex hull; they are not point masses.

Half-turn symmetry cancels the filled y and z moments. It does **not** cancel the x moment, prove convexity beyond the hull definition, or prove that only one stable equilibrium remains. Those are separate requirements.

### 3.3 Exact filled-mass closure inside a finite contact cell

In the selected closure charts, every moving input point has velocity parallel to the z-axis. Every four-point orientation determinant is therefore affine in the closure parameter s: terms containing two parallel velocity columns vanish.

On a fixed actual hull/contact interval, an outward surface triangulation gives

`V(s)=Σdet(a,b,c)/6`,

`Mx(s)=Σdet(a,b,c)(ax+bx+cx)/24`.

The x-coordinates are fixed in this chart, so both V and Mx are affine in s. The other two moments vanish by the exact half-turn pairing. Thus an exact rational zero of Mx closes **all three actual filled first moments**, provided the root remains in the certified hull cell.

That proviso was checked for the retained exact bodies: the root’s actual hull was rebuilt, its maximal facets were recovered, and the ordinary filled moments were reintegrated. Formal affine polynomial values outside a contact cell are not interpreted as physical hull moments there.

A related finite correction moves one existing mirrored shadow pair by opposite z offsets. Its admissibility is tested against the same ascent and supporting geometry, and its exact filled-moment root is taken only inside the checked cell. This is integrated material compensation, not an external ballast approximation.
