## A. Central disk and perpendicular slab force two strict stable facets

Let a full-dimensional convex polytope K contain the origin in its interior.
For a unit vector e, suppose K contains the disk of radius R in e-perp and lies
in the slab |e.x| <= h, with R>h>0. Its continuous radial function rho on the
unit sphere is at least R on the equator and at most h at either pole.
On each closed hemisphere rho therefore achieves a minimum away from the equator.
The two corresponding boundary points are distinct local distance minima.

Every such local minimum p is in the relative interior of a unique maximal facet.
To prove this without a nondegeneracy assumption, consider an active support a.x<=b.
If a is not parallel to p, let v=a-(a.p)p/|p|^2. Then p.v=0 and a.v=|v|^2>0.
For fixed C>|v|^2/(2|p|^2), x=p+epsilon*v-C*epsilon^2*p is outside that support
and has norm less than |p| for sufficiently small positive epsilon. The segment
from 0 to x meets the boundary at a point tending to p with still smaller norm,
a contradiction. Hence every active normal is a positive multiple of p.
Distinct maximal facets cannot then meet at p. The orthogonal foot p is strictly
inside a single maximal facet, and the tangential Hessian of squared distance is
positive definite. Thus both hemisphere minima are strict stable equilibria.

For Finch C182 choose n=(47,17,0), n.n=2498, e=n/sqrt(2498),
R=3/40 and h=1/56. Their ratio is 21/5.
For every exact centred vertex x, the checker proves

    1 - (n.x)^2 / ((n.n)*h^2) > 1/100.

For every one of the 136 supplied raw supports, put d=b-a.c. The checker proves
that d>0 and

    1 - R^2*((n.n)*(a.a)-(a.n)^2) / ((n.n)*d^2) > 1/100.

The first group proves the slab inclusion by convexity. The second proves the
central-disk inclusion by maximising a.x on that disk. No square root or floating
comparison is needed. The limiting vertex in the first group is raw-lex 27;
the limiting supplied support in the second is row 27. These are different
index spaces even though the two displayed integers coincide.
This applies the journal's previously established disk/slab lemma to C182;
it is not a claim that this general lemma is new.

## B. Central segment and coaxial cylinder force two strict unstable vertices

For a unit vector f, suppose K contains the segment [-R*f,R*f] and lies in the
cylinder |proj_(f-perp) x| <= h, with R>h>0. Now rho is at most h on the equator
and at least R at both poles. On each closed hemisphere it achieves a maximum
away from the equator. These give distinct local maxima of boundary distance.

A boundary local maximum of squared Euclidean distance must be a vertex:
otherwise its minimal face contains a two-sided line segment through the point,
and strict convexity of squared distance makes one side increase.
At a vertex p, every incident edge vector d must satisfy p.d<0. Indeed, if p.d>=0,
then |p+t*d|^2=|p|^2+2t*p.d+t^2*|d|^2 increases for every sufficiently small t>0,
contradicting local maximality. The vertex tangent cone is generated by these
incident edge vectors, so the plane with normal p supports K only at p.
The maximum is therefore a strict unstable equilibrium, not a degenerate substitute.

For Finch C182 use f=(0,0,1), R=1/4 and h=1/8, with ratio 2.
All 136 supplied supports satisfy

    1 - (1/4)*abs(a_z)/(b-a.c) > 1/20,

which includes both segment endpoints. All 54 centred vertices satisfy

    1 - (x_x^2+x_y^2)/(1/8)^2 > 3/100,

which includes the entire convex body in the cylinder. The limiting supplied
support is row 121; the limiting raw-lex vertex is 49.
The conclusion is U>=2, not an independently inferred complete census.

## C. Quantitative necessary shape change

Let L be another polytope translated by its OWN exact filled centroid, and let
alpha,beta>0 satisfy alpha*K subseteq L subseteq beta*K.
Then L contains a disk of radius alpha*(3/40) and is in a perpendicular slab
of halfheight beta/56. Therefore beta/alpha<21/5 implies S(L)>=2.
Likewise beta/alpha<2 implies U(L)>=2.
These results allow arbitrary changes of incidence and edge count.

In the symmetric sandwich (1-epsilon)K subseteq L subseteq (1+epsilon)K,
0<=epsilon<1, the corresponding exclusions are epsilon<8/13 for monostability
and epsilon<1/3 for mono-instability. This is a multiplicative containment bound,
not a Hausdorff-distance bound or a percentage of coordinate movement.

For an invertible linear map A, the uniform-volume centroid transforms as A*c.
The image of the central disk contains a disk in the transformed central plane
of radius at least sigma_min(A)*R. The transformed slab has halfheight
h/|A^(-T)e| <= sigma_max(A)*h. Thus any affine deformation of C182 with Euclidean
spectral condition number kappa_2(A)<21/5 is necessarily at least bistable.
The segment/cylinder argument similarly excludes mono-instability for kappa_2(A)<2.
These are necessary bounds only: exceeding them is not a construction of own110.
No global exclusion of C182 or of C<622 is claimed.
